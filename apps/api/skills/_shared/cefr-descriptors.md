## CEFR levels for Arabic reading and writing

Calibrate difficulty and assessment against these. They describe what a learner
at each level can do, not how many words they know.

| Level | Reading | Writing |
|---|---|---|
| **A1** | Isolated familiar words, very short simple sentences, high-frequency vocabulary. Needs full vocalisation. | Single words and formulaic phrases. Copies and adapts simple models. |
| **A2** | Short simple texts on familiar topics. Finds specific information in everyday material. Handles simple past and present. | Short simple sentences joined by و / ثم. Describes immediate surroundings and routine. |
| **B1** | Straightforward factual texts on familiar subjects. Follows narrative sequence. Handles common subordination. | Connected text on familiar topics. Narrates events, gives reasons, uses لأن / لكن / عندما. |
| **B2** | Articles and reports on contemporary issues. Follows argument. Handles the passive, complex iḍāfa chains, and most subordination. | Clear detailed text. Develops an argument, weighs positions, uses cohesive devices deliberately. |
| **C1** | Long complex factual and literary texts, appreciating register shifts. Handles implicit meaning and classical echoes in modern prose. | Well-structured text on complex subjects, controlling register and rhetorical effect. |
| **C2** | Virtually all forms, including abstract, structurally complex, and highly literary or classical text. | Coherent, fluent, stylistically appropriate writing, with precise nuance. |

### Calibration rules
- Judge against **what the learner did**, not what a native speaker would do.
- A learner who conveys the meaning with imperfect grammar is demonstrating
  comprehension. Score content and language separately; never let a spelling
  slip suppress a correct answer.
- When evidence is thin, prefer the **lower** adjacent level and say the
  confidence is low. An inflated estimate makes every later measurement wrong.
